# France Talent Passport Deck Profile

This profile encodes the user-supplied outline for a France Talent Passport startup deck. Treat it as a required presentation structure, not as proof of official legal or immigration requirements. Do not claim eligibility or approval; use the current official authority for any legal or program claim.

## Structure

Preserve 18–19 slides:

| Slide | Required content | Evidence and preferred visual |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | Company, plain-language one-liner, founder/contact; minimal composition |
| 2 | Problem | Specific French or target customer, current workaround, quantified cost or consequence |
| 3 | Product and solution: value | Product category, user benefit, and unique approach |
| 4 | Product and solution: experience | Real screenshot, annotated user journey, prototype, or before/after |
| 5 | Product and solution: proof | Actual traction or validation metrics with timeframes; label estimates and targets |
| 6 | Product and solution: technology | Technical mechanism, proprietary element, dependencies, and defensibility |
| 7 | Product and solution: maturity | Current product stage, completed evidence, remaining validation, and next proof point |
| 8 | Innovation | What is technically, commercially, or operationally new versus the status quo |
| 9 | Society, ecology, France, sustainability | Specific beneficiaries, measurable contribution, baseline, and target; omit unsupported ESG language |
| 10 | Competitors | Direct, indirect, and status-quo alternatives; evidence-based differences and a compact SWOT only if required |
| 11 | Target customer profile | Segment, buyer/user, job, pain, buying trigger, willingness to pay, and reachable channel |
| 12 | Market | Bottom-up TAM/SAM/SOM or equivalent addressable-demand calculation |
| 13 | Market, optional | Growth, segmentation, adoption, or timing only when evidence warrants a second slide |
| 13–14 | Why France | France-specific demand, beneficiaries, ecosystem fit, localization, jobs, capabilities, or strategic contribution that the project can substantiate |
| 14–15 | Business model | Customer, payer, price, unit, gross economics, sales motion, and expansion logic |
| 15–16 | Three-year business plan | Annual summary sourced only from the supporting workbook; show scenario and units |
| 16–17 | Three-year roadmap | Dated product, commercial, hiring, regulatory, and impact milestones tied to funding |
| 17–18 | Founders | Photo, role, relevant accomplishments, founder-market fit, LinkedIn, contacts, and languages |
| 18–19 | Closing | Clear next step and contact; do not repeat the whole deck |

If the market uses one slide, shift subsequent numbers down and deliver 18 slides. If it uses two, deliver 19. Do not add or remove another section without approval.

## France Research Priority

Use the most relevant current primary sources:

1. Official company records and customer evidence
2. French and EU statistics such as INSEE, Eurostat, and data.gouv.fr
3. Applicable ministry, regulator, Legifrance, or EU authority
4. ADEME or other competent authority for environmental claims
5. Bpifrance, Business France, France 2030, or the responsible program owner for ecosystem and innovation context
6. Competitor product, pricing, filing, and documentation sources

Use industry reports only when the primary source is unavailable or the report owns the underlying methodology. Record access dates and avoid repeating one upstream estimate as multiple confirmations.

## Profile-Specific Quality Rules

- Explain France relevance through concrete demand, execution, and measurable contribution; flags and generic praise are not evidence.
- Separate innovation from ordinary feature differentiation.
- Put sustainability claims into operational metrics that the project can measure.
- Tie the three-year plan and roadmap to the same model scenario and funding ask.
- Keep French or English terminology consistent with the requested output language; retain official program terms where translation would create ambiguity.
